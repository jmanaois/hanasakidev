notes & observations / october 22, 2026

spring, through a new lens

i've been to japan before, in the heavy heat of summer and the crisp quiet of winter. but this spring was the first time i went with a camera i actually knew how to use, and the first time i went when the whole country seemed to be in bloom. it ended up being less of a trip and more of a reminder of why i fell for photography, and for japan, in the first place.

a country in bloom

there's no way around it: cherry blossom season changes everything. in summer, japan feels like it's moving fast. people duck from one air-conditioned doorway to the next, and the days are loud with cicadas. winter is the opposite, still and clear and a little inward. spring sits somewhere in between. everyone slows down at the same time. parks fill up with picnic blankets, people stop mid-sidewalk to take a picture of a branch, and whole afternoons get spent doing nothing but sitting under the trees.

what surprised me most was how short it is. the blossoms come and go in about a week or two, and you can feel that everyone knows it. there's a kind of shared attention to it, a collective "look at this while it's here," that i haven't felt anywhere else.

finally getting to know my camera

this was the trip where my fujifilm x-t30 iii stopped being a new purchase and started being a habit. i kept it on the 23mm f/2.8 almost the whole time. having one small, quiet lens meant i stopped fiddling with settings and started paying attention to what was in front of me instead.

most of what i ended up loving wasn't the landmarks. it was my friends laughing on the walk between stations, someone stopping to tie a shoe, a stranger on a bridge crouched down to get the perfect shot of their own. little glimpses into other people's days, the kind of moments you'd normally walk right past. the camera gave me an excuse to notice them.

new corners of kansai

i also got to see parts of the kansai region i'd never been to before. walking somewhere for the first time with a camera in hand is a different experience. i found myself lingering at shrine gates, following side paths just to see where they went, and waiting for someone to walk through a scene so it would feel lived in.

somewhere along the way i realized how much i'd missed this. not just taking photos, but the curiosity that comes with it. reading signs i could only half understand, learning the small rituals at each shrine, being reminded how much care goes into everyday things in japan. it rekindled something i'd let go quiet for a while, both behind the camera and in my love for the culture.

next up: autumn

in november, i'm going back. this time it'll be my first trip in the fall, and i honestly can't wait. i want to return to some of the places from this spring and see them in a completely different color, with red maples where the blossoms were and cooler air on the same streets. i also want to find somewhere entirely new.

i'm bringing the x-t30 iii, of course. if spring taught me anything, it's to slow down, keep the camera close, and pay attention to the people passing through. i'll share what i find when i'm back.
